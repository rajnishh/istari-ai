How it keeps the Mac from freezing

Watch it step in, before the beach ball

  1. It spots the first signMemory pressure, or swap growing 1 GB in two minutes. That's how nearly every freeze starts.
  2. It slows, it doesn't killThe program that grew 1.5 GB in two minutes moves to background priority. It keeps working, and your other apps stay responsive.
  3. It puts things backOnce memory has been fine for two minutes. One alert says what it did and names the biggest apps.

Free memory now

One key press frees what's safe. The rest is your call.

Press ⌃⌥⌘F anywhere. Warden slows whatever is ballooning and stops idle AI servers and unused simulators. It never touches your work. If that isn't enough, it lists what else you could stop, with how much each frees.

After your agents

Tidies up when a session ends, and writes down why

Warden knows which coding session started each dev server, model server and simulator. When the session ends and they've gone idle, they're stopped, and every stop goes into a plain file.

  • Downloads, training jobs and terminals are never touched.
  • Your agents can read STOPPED-BY-WARDEN.md, so they don't "fix" a stop by starting it again.

Disk

Frees disk without losing the story

Each agent's worktree installs its own packages, often 2 GB or more. When a worktree is truly finished, Warden clears them. Before it removes a folder, it saves a memory card: the PR, commits, files changed and the conversations that made it.

  • Kept if anything changed, is running, or could exist nowhere else (a .env, downloaded models).
  • Reopen makes the worktree again and continues its conversation.

What it does

  • 01

    No more freezes

    At the first sign of trouble (memory pressure, or swap growing 1 GB in two minutes), Warden slows the program that's ballooning. It keeps working, and your other apps stay responsive.

  • 02

    A cool Mac

    When the Mac runs hot or CPU stays high, the busiest programs move to background priority, then come back once it's calm.

  • 03

    No leftover servers

    Warden knows which coding session started each dev server. When the session ends and the server goes idle, it's stopped, so port 3000 is free again.

  • 04

    No forgotten models

    Local AI servers left idle and simulators nobody is using are stopped, if you turn that on. Ollama, Docker and databases are protected.

  • 05

    Room on the disk

    Worktrees whose work is merged and quiet lose their packages, then the folder, after a memory card is saved. Reopen brings any of them back.

  • 06

    Servers and tunnels that stay up

    Dev servers, workers and tunnels restart on their own, with health checks from inside and outside. A copy you started in Terminal is adopted, not doubled.

Safe by design

The rules every part of Warden follows.

Off until you say so

Anything that stops a program or deletes files starts off. Until you turn it on, Warden only suggests it, with a button to do it once.

Says what you'd lose

Every Stop, Quit and Clear shows its risk, and the confirmation shows it again. Even when the risk is small.

Leaves live work alone

It never quits an app, closes a window or force quits on its own. Editors, agents, terminals and protected programs are never stopped.

Writes down why

Every stop, clear and removal goes into a file with what, why and how to get it back, so you and your agents always know.
